You choose the experiment

Predict. Change one thing. Explain.

  1. Predict: If you raise the brightness cutoff, will more or fewer pixels remain white?
  2. Test: Compare cutoffs 100, 150, and 200 on the same image. Record the white-pixel percentages.
  3. Explain: Which bright objects survive besides the ball? Why is a white pixel not evidence of a ball?
  4. Extend: Try grayscale or blur. Can you describe information each operation removes?

Ask for a test, then run it yourself

I am testing brightness thresholding on a soccer image. First ask me to predict what a higher threshold will do. Suggest one fair comparison and one example that might fool the filter. Do not invent results or identify a player. Wait for my measurements, then help me explain a limitation and plan the next test.

Research connection: a ball tracker must connect observations across frames. This lab processes one image at a time; it does not track motion or stabilize video.

The brightness rule uses weighted RGB values (21.26% red, 71.52% green, 7.22% blue), without linear-light conversion. Box blur averages RGB values inside a square window, clipped at image edges. Mean RGB change is the average absolute before/after difference across all color channels. It measures change, not image quality or model accuracy.

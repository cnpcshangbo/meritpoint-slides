Preflight 1 of 4 · supported claim

Quick check · tap an answer

A candidate model outperformed the baseline on the same predefined folds, but only one dataset was tested. Which headline is defensible?

Bound the claim to the data and protocol actually tested. A stronger-sounding sentence is not a stronger result.

Quick vote only. This is a preflight ownership check, not the start of another lecture.
Preflight · the sentence test

Can the whole study fit in one honest sentence?

For [population / dataset scope], we tested whether [one change] affected [pre-chosen metric] versus [baseline], using [saved protocol]; we found [bounded result], limited by [largest threat].

Names the comparison

Change, baseline and outcome are visible.

Names the protocol

The audience knows how the test stayed fair.

Names the boundary

The claim stops where the evidence stops.

Give presenters thirty seconds to say their sentence to a partner. If it cannot fit, the deck is probably trying to defend more than one study.
Deck rule · maximum six slides

Six slides, including the title

1 · Question

Title + bounded research question.

2 · Data

Source, version, n, scope and exclusions.

3 · Method

Change, baseline, controls and protocol.

4 · Results

Full comparison + uncertainty/error view.

5 · Meaning

Conclusion, limitations and rival explanation.

6 · Reproduce

Notebook/data links, AI disclosure, next test.

Six is a ceiling, not a target. Merge or cut anything that does not help the audience audit the claim.
This slide is the only deck template students need. The title counts. Appendix slides are not a loophole; supporting details belong in the linked notebook or paper.

Student slide 3 · method

Show two lanes so the one change is obvious

Comparison contract

Baseline · one intended change · controls · pre-chosen metric.

Protocol receipt

Saved folds, seed, model settings, preprocessing boundary and threshold rule.

Leakage defense

Fit learned preprocessing inside each training fold—never on all rows before validation.

Preflight 2 of 4 · fair test

Quick check · tap an answer

The candidate uses a new feature set and a new train/test split; the baseline uses the old features and old split. The candidate wins. What can the presenter defend?

Both the feature set and evaluation rows changed. Re-run contenders on the same saved protocol before attributing the difference.

Quick vote. Ask students to name both changed variables; then move on.
Student slide 4 · results

Give one focal comparison—and keep the receipts nearby

Focal visual

Put baseline and contender adjacent. Label metric, units, sample count and evaluation protocol.

Variation

Show every fold or an honest center plus spread; keep failed and weak runs visible.

Reading sentence

Say what the chart establishes, then stop before mechanism or outside generalization.

Preflight 3 of 4 · honest headline

Quick check · tap an answer

A candidate catches more positives but also creates more false alarms than the baseline. Which headline is honest?

Name the trade-off and connect it to the cost rule chosen before results. A headline should not erase the metric that became inconvenient.

Quick vote only. Students should be ready to say which stakeholder bears each error.
Student slide 5 · meaning

Conclusion + limits belong on the same screen

Supported conclusion

Answer the question using the observed direction, size and uncertainty.

Largest limitation

Name the threat most likely to change the result — not a generic “more data.”

Rival explanation

What else could have produced the pattern, and what evidence would separate it?

Example · three different sentences
ConclusionOn these fixed penguin folds, scaled 5-NN averaged 98.5% versus 79.5%.
BoundaryOne dataset, one classifier setting and one fold assignment do not establish a universal scaling benefit.
Next testRepeat the frozen comparison across other datasets, k values and fold seeds.
Contrast the three example sentences. The first reports the observed comparison, the second blocks overreach, and the third turns uncertainty into an experiment.
Student slide 6 · reproduce

Finish with a compact receipt, not a wall of code

Reproduction card

Code/version · environment · data identity · seed/folds · settings · saved outputs.

AI-use disclosure

What the tool generated, what you changed, and which outputs you independently reran and checked.

One next test

Name an analysis that could challenge the conclusion—not decoration that assumes it is already true.

Example · reproducibility footer

From data loader to both accuracy receipts

data: palmer-penguins.csv @ 8957207 (342 complete × 4) features: bill length/depth · flipper length · body mass cv: StratifiedKFold(5, shuffle=True, seed=15) model: KNeighborsClassifier(k=5) change: StandardScaler fitted inside each training fold metric: accuracy · sklearn 1.9.0

AI-use example: AI helped draft the slide wording; the code was rerun and every displayed value was checked against saved output.

A file, cell, query or calculation should answer “where did this number come from?”

The receipt is short enough to read and specific enough to rerun. It demonstrates disclosure as a verification record rather than a tool logo.

Preflight 4 of 4 · unknown answer

Quick check · tap an answer

A teacher asks whether the result holds for a subgroup the project never analyzed. What is the strongest response?

Ownership includes knowing where the evidence ends. State the boundary and a valid next test instead of improvising certainty.

Last preflight vote. After this, no more quizzes: move into question roles, rubric and live showcase controls.
Teacher card · minimum two questions

One validity question + one meaning/reproduction question

Lane A · validity

  • What changed, and what stayed fixed?
  • How did you prevent leakage?
  • Why does the split match real use?
  • What baseline had to be beaten?
  • Was the metric chosen before results?

Lane B · interpretation / reproduction

  • What evidence would reverse your conclusion?
  • Which limitation matters most?
  • Can another student rerun this exactly?
  • What AI output did you verify or reject?
  • What is the next test, and why?
Ask at least one from each lane. Questions test ownership of the study, not trivia or public-speaking style.
Keep both required questions inside the two-minute Q&A. If time remains, invite one peer clarification, but do not displace the validity and interpretation/reproduction checks.
Peer listener card

Glow · Grow · Next test

Glow

The strongest supported claim or clearest evidence link.

Grow

One place where question → method → result → conclusion was unclear.

Next test

One feasible experiment that could challenge the claim or address its largest limit.

“Glow: I trusted ___ because ___. Grow: clarify ___. Next test: hold ___ fixed and test ___.”
Feedback is about evidence, never personality, accent or identity. Peers write during Q&A and finish during the one-minute transition.

Audience test · design backward

What should a reviewer know in ten seconds?

1 · Your direction

“I investigate how data and models answer questions in ___.”

2 · Your best project

A title plus one sentence explaining the question and what you built.

3 · Your evidence

One readable result, one artifact link and one honest limitation.

Ten-second test: cover your name. Can a stranger still tell what problem you studied, what you did, and why the result is credible?
Give a fast silent scan. The page is for a busy teacher, mentor, admissions reader or internship reviewer—not for someone who already knows the course.

Pick the simplest platform you can keep updated

Fastest · no code

Google Sites, Notion or Canva website.
Best when tomorrow's goal is a working link.

Code-forward

GitHub Pages.
Best when the repository itself is part of the evidence.

Private / reviewer-only

PDF or access-controlled page.
Use when age, policy, licensing or data sensitivity makes public sharing inappropriate.

Platform choice is not the achievement. A clear project card on a simple page beats an elaborate site with no evidence.
Do not require a public identity or account. Students may use a teacher-approved alias, reviewer-only link or PDF. The course Portfolio Launchpad can export one HTML file for the GitHub Pages lane.
One-page architecture · four reading depths

Make the page useful at a glance—and on inspection

Top · 10 seconds

Professional display name or approved alias · one-line focus · one featured project.

Project card · 30 seconds

Question · your role · methods/tools · supported result · limitation.

Evidence · 2 minutes

Chart with caption · notebook/repository · short report/deck · reproducibility note.

Next step · 10 seconds

What you learned · next test · safe contact or application route.

The architecture supports skimming and auditing. Keep each layer self-contained; do not make the reviewer hunt through a repository to discover the research question.

Featured project · the six-line card

Turn the mini-study into a reviewer-facing story

  1. Question: What did you try to learn?
  2. Role: What did you personally do?
  3. Method: Data, baseline and one controlled change.
  1. Evidence: What did the fair comparison show?
  2. Boundary: What can it not establish?
  3. Next test: What would reduce the uncertainty?
Write for a reader who has not taken this class. Expand acronyms once. Replace “we used AI” with the exact task, tool and verification.
The six-line card reuses the defense structure. Personal role matters for team projects. Evidence should be a bounded result, not a list of libraries.
Evidence gallery · one image, three receipts

Show less—and explain each item more

Focal result

One chart or table with metric, units, comparison and a self-contained caption.

Reproduce

Notebook/repository + data identity + environment or “how to rerun” note.

Read further

Short report or ≤6-slide deck for the reasoning and limitations.

Link hygiene: descriptive link text, working permissions, stable filenames, no “click here,” no credentials, and no raw personal record.
Explain that screenshots of code are not reproducible artifacts. The chart earns its place only if the caption states what it shows and the project card states what it does not show.
Reflection · make growth visible

The most interesting sentence may be what changed

I first assumed…initial model of the problem
→
The evidence showed…result or failure that changed it
→
So I revised…claim, method or next question

Strong reflection

Names a concrete decision, evidence and revision.

Weak reflection

“I learned a lot about AI” with no trace of what changed.

For students without a dramatic positive result, process and revision are still strong evidence of research maturity. A null result can anchor an excellent reflection.

Resume · turn nouns into evidence

Action + artifact + method + evidence + boundary

Designed and evaluated [project / model] on [named data] using [fair-comparison method]; found [bounded result], documented [limitation], and published a reproducible project page.

Avoid

“Used Python and AI for a machine-learning project.”

Prefer

A verb, a concrete artifact, the comparison you owned and the scope of the evidence.

Do not force a numeric improvement when the result was null or the metric is not meaningful out of context. The Portfolio Launchpad generates a first draft, but the student must edit it for truth and readability.

Before publish · public, private or remove

Every item needs an audience decision

Public-safe by defaultReviewer-only unless approvedRemove
Aggregate charts · original explanations · licensed/project-owned images · de-identified code Full notebook with controlled data · manuscript under review · team artifacts · age/school details Passwords/API keys · raw personal records · private emails · precise address/schedule · unlicensed data
Minors: follow family, school and program rules. A professional alias, private link or PDF is a valid portfolio—not a lesser one.

Peer QA · five-minute scan test

Review the page as a stranger, not as a classmate

10 seconds

Say the problem, the student's role and the strongest evidence—without asking the author.

2 minutes

Open every link. Check access, labels, captions, mobile readability and private information.

Final note

Write one Glow, one missing receipt and one sentence to cut or clarify.

Pass condition: the reviewer can explain the claim and its boundary, then reach the evidence in two clicks or fewer.
Use pairs in the live room. In the recording, students can simulate the stranger by leaving the page for a moment, returning on a phone-size window, and reading only headings and captions.
